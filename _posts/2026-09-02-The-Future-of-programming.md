It's been a long time since I wrote anything here or uploaded an entry. I didn't see any value in maintaining a site like this, with valuable information on it, because AI will replace anyone who programs, yada yada.
However, I noticed a shift: there have been postings, especially on Reddit, that promote an AI detox, which promotes creativity again. We see a general concern among the public regarding the AI industry (*Hugging Face*).
A lot of people also notice that the companies that accelerate the AI industry are genuinely evil, see [this ponzi scheme](https://ai-circular-economy.com/) by these companies.
But what about us (undergrad) Computer Scientists? *Gen Z speak* Are we cooked chat? Chat this real?
No, the influx of AI in the industry will, with the help of AI, produce an enormous pile of unfixed, gibberish, and zero-optimized code. *You will need humans to fix this crap.*
But it is also a fact that AI will evaporate these "programmers" who don't value programming, i.e., the web-dev (frontend) or the average CS grad from the USA [American CS grads aren't comparable with their European or Asian counterparts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2AJH_EZhR8g&t=886s).
You have to keep in mind that this guy is also farming clicks with this content, but the general pattern is this: the graduates are simply "mass produced," they are able to fix code or write smaller applications, but they don't strive for knowledge nor a deeper understanding.
It all boils down to one thing: AI won't replace the programmer per se, it will replace the performative programmer, the one who has a MacBook, drinks a latte, and studies CS because *muh* creativity.
However, AI which is solely trained on specific data, for example, math, biology, or human medicine, is a great tool; it is a great tool for static analysis of low-level code. I repeat, it is just a *tool* and that's it.
General-purpose LLMs are nothing more than a con trick.
Like DHH said in the [Lex Fridman podcast](https://youtu.be/hO-i1l8BecM?t=94), vibe coding is nothing more than what script kiddies did before LLMs.
But what is my take on AI (LLM)?
Genuinely, don't use it. If I use it, I mainly use it to write letters to a government agency, for example. As in programming, if I do low-level stuff [see](https://github.com/ProMathX/osue-extended), I use it for static analysis; this means, if I have faulty code, asan and valgrind behave normally, the LSP doesn't detect an error, and the manual debugging with err and the step-by-step debugging with gdb doesn't seem to resolve the issue, I use Claude and only tell it the line where it might be faulty. I read every slop response with care. Normally in such cases, it is an off-by-one error, for example, I forgot to add +1 to a buffer.
